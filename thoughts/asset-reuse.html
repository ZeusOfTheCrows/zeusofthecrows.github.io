--- title: "Reduce, Reuse, Recycle" subtitle: "The Advantages of Reusing Assets Between Video Games" layout: opinion css-file: article date: 2023-11-29 edit: 2023-11-29 ---

so i've finally played the "zelda classics", ocarina of time and majora's mask. now, i'm not going into my [controversial] thoughts on them here;* it has reminded me how much i love reused assets in games. i've never really liked d.l.c. – it usually feels either tacked on and ruins the balance of a game, or it feels like it was brutally excised from a finished game in order to make more money – but i am a huge fan of "expand-alones" (e.g. the lost and the damned expansion for grand theft auto: iv). and honestly i think there should be more games that are made with that design principle, but are the length of full games

now i'm not talking about asset reuse within a game. as much as i understand that sometimes it's necessary; i do find it rather immersion breaking when there's three completely identical "wrecked houseboats" right next to each other. this is one of the many reasons i liked far: changing tides considerably less than the excellent far: lone sails

instead, i'm talking about reuse of assets, mechanics, and engines between games. not only do i personally enjoy seeing how they've been used (it's weird seeing a mario reference in link's awakening become a ranch owner in ocarina of time, then the same model as a barkeep in majora's mask); i believe it also actually makes for better games in general

having a considerable amount of work already done when starting with making a game allows a much faster development cycle, which also means getting original games cheaper; which allows companies make games that otherwise wouldn't get made as they're too much of a financial risk (again, see majora's mask)

when a new game comes out, the question is always what is its u.s.p./u.p.d.?; but i think the better games often come from situations when all the hard work has been done already

not only that, but the games made with said re-used assets are very often better than the games those assets were originally made for. exempli gratia:

majora's mask
such a great reuse of ocarina of time assets. most of the music, including the ocarina songs, are re-used; almost all enemy models, many npc and item models, sound effects, etc.
tears of the kingdom
like mm, almost everything in totk comes from breath of the wild. i realise there could be a lot of zelda games on this list, but i had to include totk. i really enjoyed botw when it came out, but to my mind it's completely obsolete now. totk has superceded it in every way
fallout: new vegas
fallout 3. now admittedly i haven't actually played either of these, so i can't write much here. but i'm aware f:nv is generally considered the better game
portal
half-life 2 - now admittedly it doesn't use that much from hl2 apart from the engine, but it would never have been developed without the existence of hl2. see the early betas* (video) for the assets used in prototyping and developing portal, and they all come from half-life (mainly nova prospekt). subjectively better than hl2, but i like it more
dark souls
as much as i love it, demon's souls is almost a prototype for dark souls. apart from re-using many non-unique weapon & item models, it feels like a testbed for dark souls, which did indeed proceed to rectify almost all of demon's souls' mistakes
grand theft auto: vice city
gta 3 was an incredible technical revelation for the gta series (and open world games in general), but it's scarcely thought of as the best in the series. vice city (apart from an, in my opinion, worse map) uses gta3's framework to make a much better game. gta:sa also uses the engine, but makes so many technical changes i don't think it counts

*ocarina of time is the worst 3d zelda game i've played, and i would recommend it to absolutely no-one except avid zelda fans. fight me. *i know it says TBD, but there are files available for download; they're just a little janky
