Now, this isn't the sort of niche franchise i'd normally like to draw attention to – i'm sure most everyone has heard of Metroid, and probably played at least one – but i have a couple of unusual opinions on particular entries that i feel people should appreciate more (yes, including that one). Let me at least offset it by saying it is not just morally correct, it is a moral imperative to pirate nintendo games.

Super Metroid

I'm not sure there's much i need to say about Super. If you've played any Metroid, you'll have played Super. If you haven't, start here. If you have, and haven't played Super – why?

Metroid Dread

I love how "clean" Samus is in dread. Sure, she looks nonchalant when just walking around, but she just instantly snaps to perfect and steady aim when in danger

One can tell she's been a bounty hunter for many years at this point, and her skills and instincts are finely honed to her trade

And interestingly, this contrasts nicely with Other M, which is at the other end of her story, where she's fresh faced out of the academy and, though competent, clearly not experienced yet

All the little details that you love from dread are there (examples), on a fucking handheld from 2011

Metroid: Other M

Look, i know everyone loves to hate Other M. But after playing it, i can only assume that most people who mock it online haven't actually played it. It's not the best Metroid game, but heck it's honestly pretty good and i think more people should play it

It may not be as open as some Metroid games, but it does have a certain amount of puzzle-solving/secret-finding. But more importantly, it just feels really nice to play? being the progenitor of the flashy movement in Samus Returns/Dread, it feels so smooth and yet precise compared to the sprite-based Metroids. Samus' movement has always been a little weird (in Super, for example, one's standing jump is not quite as high as one's spin jump. This is very annoying.), but in Other M i think it might be the second nicest in the whole series, after Dread

It may make it a little too easy (Yes it does – it's an incredibly easy game. Too easy, some might say.), but the auto-aiming is really nice. Aiming in sprite-based Metroids was always a little awkward – NEStroid didn't have a crouch, and most enemies were 1 tile high; therefore completely unshootable! Yes it got better from there, but one was always limited to 45° until Samus Returns. The auto-aiming just makes the whole game so fluid

The boss fights (such as they are, i'm including mini-bosses as otherwise the game has i think …3?) are so fast-paced as well. Nightmare was the point in Fusion that i realised exactly why i didn't like that game, whereas Nightmare in Other M is actually a really good boss. Not one boss fight takes longer than about a minute ¿i think?, and they're frantic bursts of a mini-puzzle and execution-check blended together so well. This isn't true, i just remembered the fucking forklift. But apart from that one, they're good

Look, i'm not going to deny it has its flaws. Plenty of them. But i do think that its strengths outweigh its weaknesses. There's an old phrase that players know what they don't like, but are wrong about the solutions. I think a lot of many people's complaints are a little misplaced:

The story's bad

The execution is. It's really, really bad. The writing is bad, the voice acting is worse, and the inner monologue is a perfect storm combining the two

Furthermore, the whole plot point of the "deleter", and the whole big conspiracy about which member of the squad he was, was just dropped? One can work out his identity by paying real close attention, but it aint that obvious; and considering how much game focusses on it, i really feels like you should be able to fight him? or at least report him to the GalFed?

But i don't think the high concept is necessarily bad. It's a direction for Samus' character that i personally don't like, and it's ncongruous with her previous characterisation in the prime series (although it's not too out of keeping with Fusion, and the ending of II/Samus Returns); but i think in isolation it's not too bad a concept to consider Samus' feelings toward an engineered bioweapon: the metroid she disobeyed her mission brief to save – souring her relationship with the galactic federation – and which later saved her life against mother brain; and consider it via the allegory of an engineered bioweapon: an artificial intelligence designed to control other bioform weapons and the parental feelings toward it by its creator

It's too linear to be a metroid game

Despite their reputation, and the genre named after them, the mainline metroid games aren't really that non-linear…? nestroid obviously is, as are super and the prime series

But Other M is probably on a par with II, Zero Mission, and to a lesser extent Samus Returns. In fact, it's probably less linear than Fusion. That's over half the metroid series at the point it came out. If you approach it as a metroidvania, it's restricting. If you approach it as a metroid game, it's fine. Would it be improved by removing all the unnecessarily locked doors? I think so, yes. But on the other hand you'd be more likely to get lost, so it's not a blanket improvement. I do wish there was an option for that though, even if as a hard mode after one playthrough, à la fusion mode

The controls are bad

I mean yeah, probably? it was on the god-forsaken Wii! That thing has about as many buttons as a fucking NES controller! I played it with a DS5, which i think was the ideal controller for it: soft mushy DPad for smooth movement, enough buttons for all situations, and decent gyro for aim mode. Yes, it would have been improved by nunchuck support, but i imagine they were trying to reduce the barrier to entry by not requiring it

I have no defence for the shoulder-cam though, apart from that it was in vogue at the the time due to Resident Evil IV. I guess they're trying to make the game more cinematic (derogatory), but it doesn't need to be there

3D movement needs an analogue stick

Related to the previous complaint, but i want to address it separately as i have opinions™. Yes, this sounds bad on paper, but i think Team Ninja dealt with it really well. I mapped the analogue stick to movement (digital only though) on my controller, but i kept drifting back to using the DPad. At almost every point you want to be either moving sideways (like a traditional metroid) or directly away away from the camera. This makes the cardinal movement feel really nice, and i actually much prefer it to the analogue only 2D movement in Samus Returns/dread

The search view sections are dumb

Yeah. They are. They're really bad. Being locked into position and having to pixel hunt without even knowing what one's hunting for is such a fucking stupid idea i can't believe it made it into the final game. The ones nearest the beginning are the worst though for some reason? if you can suffer through

    searching for something genuinely so hidden i thought my game had softlocked. I had to fucking google it. It was completely invisible, hidden behind a plant; and the game starting you off facing the wrong way and facing something ostensibly much more important – a dead squad member – and expects you to turn 180° and look at a random bit of green splat on some green grass
then after that, they're not too bad. Still a fucking dumb concept though.

Well now i feel like i've ended on a sour note, as there are some major missteps with this game. But i do really like it! The minute to minute gameplay is genuinely really fun! There are a couple of mods to remove/revamp the story cutscenes, though i haven't tried them as i wanted to experience the game in its original form out of interest (will probably try Redux at some point though). I did however use an HD pack, as well as two gecko codes for missiles in 3rd-person and nunchuck / classic controller support, along with a custom controller layout
